Module 5

Supporting Friends

You can make a real difference for someone who’s struggling — without being their therapist.

💚 What Actually Helps

Peer support can be transformative — when done right...

Listen without fixing — Say "That sounds really hard" instead of "Have you tried..."

Validate without minimizing — Acknowledgment is not agreement. Never compare their pain to someone else’s.

Encourage professional connection — Gently suggest talking to a trusted adult. Offer to go with them.

✦

Just showing up and listening is more powerful than most people realize.

What would you do?

Your friend breaks down after school: "Everything feels pointless. I don’t know why I even bother anymore." What’s the best immediate response?

"Things aren’t that bad — you got an A last week."
Stay with them, listen without fixing, acknowledge how they feel, and gently ask if they want to talk to a counselor together.
"You should tell your parents — they can help."

⚠️ What Does Not Help

Even well-meaning responses can do harm...

"Just get over it" — Dismisses their experience and implies they should control it through willpower.

Comparing problems — "At least you don’t have..." invalidates their pain. Suffering is not a competition.

Promising secrecy about safety — If someone mentions hurting themselves, their life matters more than their temporary anger.

What would you do?

At midnight your friend texts: "I don’t think anyone would care if I wasn’t here. Don’t tell anyone — I just needed to say it." What do you do?

Respect their privacy — they said they just needed to talk
Text encouragement and check in tomorrow
Tell them you care too much to stay silent, stay in contact, and reach out to a trusted adult immediately

🚨 When to Involve Adults — and Taking Care of Yourself

Some situations are bigger than peer support...

Involve an adult immediately if your friend:

Talks about wanting to die, gives away belongings, shows sudden calm after depression, or if you’re genuinely worried — even if they ask you not to tell.

Say: "I care about you too much to keep this to myself."

Safe2Tell Wyoming (safe2tellwy.org) lets you report concerns anonymously.

You deserve care too. Supporting someone through crisis is heavy. Talk to a trusted adult about how you’re feeling. Sustainable compassion requires boundaries.

Pause and Reflect

How would you want a friend to support you if you were struggling? Advice, presence, or something else?

Most people don’t want solutions. They want to be heard.
